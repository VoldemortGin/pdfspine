## Span-seam measurements for phase F

`sample_span_seams.py` measured every adjacent pair of non-synthetic glyphs that the candidate
already placed in one span, over the same 300 documents and at most 20 pages per document. It
folds consecutive characters with the same `seq` into one glyph. The run covered 398,591 nonempty spans
and 6,212,741 seams with no extraction errors or degenerate normalizers.

For both glyphs, `normalizer = max(rendered_size)`. The recorded metrics are:

- `linear = max(abs(delta(a,b,c,d))) / normalizer`
- `dir_dot = clamp(dot(prev_dir, curr_dir), -1, 1)`; horizontal direction is normalized
  `matrix(a,b)`, while vertical text uses `line.dir`
- `baseline = abs(dot(curr.origin - prev.origin, normal(prev_dir))) / normalizer`

| Metric | Zero | Nonzero | Nonzero P50 | P90 | P95 | P99 | P99.9 | Max |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Linear | 6,203,695 | 9,046 | 0.111109 | 0.350018 | 0.353293 | 0.460000 | 0.866951 | 1.333458 |
| Baseline | 6,202,957 | 9,784 | 0.0000526 | 0.030151 | 0.137641 | 0.654105 | 1.215095 | 1.215095 |

All direction dots were at least 0.999997335. There was no reversed direction inside a current
span.

Manual review found clear split-positive examples: `MKTX_2018_page_134.pdf` page 0 merges a
10 pt table number with a 1 pt space (`linear=0.9`); `nasa-ntrs-19950009349.pdf` pages 9 and 16
mix formula/rotated glyph transforms and sizes in spans (maximum `linear=1.333458`); and the
hidden OCR layer in `govdocs1-00025.pdf` page 1 jumps about 10.8 points between adjacent glyphs
(`baseline=1.215095`). `govdocs1-00053.pdf` page 3 is a useful boundary case: visually related
table-of-contents leader dots have large matrix changes, so a strict rule can create many small
spans even when the text remains related.

The cut neighborhoods also contain ordinary prose. For example, linear 0.049757 occurs at a
space in `nasa-ntrs-19950009349.pdf` page 19, and values immediately around 0.05 occur in
`govdocs1-00018.pdf` prose. Baseline 0.05 occurs between digits in `govdocs1-00034.pdf` page 7.
This makes 0.05 a real behavioral cliff rather than a numerical-noise boundary. The ignored
`span-seams.json` contains `boundary_examples` immediately above and below every candidate cut,
plus the top 40 records, matrices, origins, sizes, contexts, fonts, pages, and seam classes.

An initial experiment can start near `linear=0.005`, `baseline=0.005`, and direction
`dot < 1 - 1e-5`, because these clear the smallest observed jitter. These are calibration inputs,
not accepted thresholds. Phase F must compare span counts and seam identities before/after,
inspect ordinary text and leader negative cases, and retain explicit positive fixtures. Word
counts alone cannot detect over-splitting because word extraction may aggregate across spans.

## Post-F 300-document posterior

The implemented thresholds were deliberately looser than the initial calibration proposal:
linear difference `<= 0.05 + 1e-9`, baseline difference `<= 0.1 + 1e-9`, and direction dot
`> 0.996`. Singular glyph pairs use exact finite linear equality and absolute baseline difference
`<= 1e-6`. The isolated post-F wheel SHA-256 was
`4f8426b4cddff4ca2938a1338b4826121320b2538e95de351e2b6c7011a241b5`.

On the frozen corpus, the post-F extractor again produced **0/300 different extraction JSON
documents** versus the pre-F candidate output. Its oracle comparison stayed exactly at 137
over-split, 329 under-split, 130 mixed, 1,887 pages, 1,246 content-equal pages, and zero errors.
This checks the word/text/font loop, while the direct span posterior below checks the behavior
that the word loop cannot see.

| Span posterior | Before F | After F | Delta |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Nonempty spans | 398,591 | 407,114 | +8,523 |
| Within-span glyph seams | 6,212,741 | 6,204,218 | -8,523 |
| Documents with a change | — | 79 / 300 | — |

The equal and opposite deltas show that exactly 8,523 formerly merged seams were split. After F,
the maximum retained linear difference was `0.05000000000000012`, the maximum retained baseline
difference was `0.1`, and the minimum retained direction dot remained `0.999997335`, consistent
with the configured thresholds and their numeric slack.

A separate page-level projection flattened every raw character while intentionally discarding
the changed span tree. Across 1,887 pages and 6,812,936 characters, the before/after SHA-256 was
equal on every page for `c`, traversal order, `seq`, `origin`, `bbox`, `matrix`, `quad`,
`rendered_size`, and `synthetic`. This is stronger than the word-loop result for glyph payload
preservation.

The largest per-document split counts were 605 (`govdocs1-00053.pdf`, dominated by leader-dot
geometry), 492 (`govdocs1-00064.pdf`), 473 (`govdocs1-00018.pdf`), 409
(`govdocs1-00056.pdf`), and 293 (`nasa-ntrs-19950009349.pdf`). Direct post-F lookup confirmed:

- the NASA page 9 `m` → `$` seam (`linear=1.333458`, `baseline=0.249585`) split;
- the hidden-OCR space seam in `govdocs1-00025.pdf` page 1 (`baseline=1.215095`) split;
- a leader-dot → space seam in `govdocs1-00053.pdf` page 3 (`linear=0.866951`) split;
- ordinary NASA page 19 prose at a space (`linear=0.049757`) stayed together;
- the `MA_2016_page_69.pdf` dot → dollar seam at baseline `0.1` stayed together;
- `govdocs1-00017.pdf` page 2 at `Sandy Pt.` → `Prisoner’s Hbr.` (`baseline=0.111130`) split.

The 605 leader-related splits in one document are the main review risk left by this threshold.
They do not change word extraction in the measured corpus, but consumers that treat each span as
a semantic run may observe more fragmentation. The explicit leader fixture and a span-count or
seam-identity gate should remain part of F's acceptance suite.

Across all documents, 420 alphabetic runs crossed at least one new boundary. Three runs became
one span per character: `КnОm` in `govdocs1-00056.pdf` page 1 (mixed-Cyrillic formula/OCR), `sitm`
in `govdocs1-00067.pdf` page 0 (OCR noise), and `HPA` in the
`300–100-HPA-LAYER` heading in `govdocs1-00073.pdf` page 5. `HPA` is semantically a normal
acronym, but its source geometry really alternates: H/A have matrix `(8,0,0,-7.5)` and rendered
size 7.746, while P has `(10,0,0,-10)` and rendered size 10. Its split is consistent with the
requested visual-span rule. No stable, same-geometry ordinary word was found split character by
character. This review does not justify describing F as free of fragmentation; it documents the
observed tradeoff and why the three strongest cases triggered.
